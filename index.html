About the data & the design

Purpose and audience. This page helps an everyday reader compare local places, routes and recorded activities, and understand broader travel context. Geographic position locates records; area and length compare counts and distances; colour compares night shares. Hover or tap marks for details, and use the labelled selectors, type buttons and map controls for keyboard access.

Four sources, different populations. City of Melbourne inventories, Victorian DEECA recreation records, Tourism Research Australia’s historical NVS and ABS geography. The Melbourne tourism region differs from the City of Melbourne and Greater Melbourne. State trips can span states and must not be added into a national total.

Methods, source vintages and reproducible files

Research snapshot: 12 October 2026. The 242-place municipal source was transparently filtered to 94 records using nine cultural/leisure subthemes. Treemap leaves count these records once. Artwork geometry points correct the reversed raw latitude/longitude columns. Recorded years are exact four-digit source values; 25 uncertain dates are excluded only from the histogram. The toilet chart selects five areas with at least ten records from all 202 works, leaving 33 outside that chart. “Melbourne remainder” abbreviates “Melbourne (Remainder)”. Every one of the selected 169 distances is plotted; vertical offsets only separate dots and encode no quantity.

Routes retain all 34 components for 11 named itineraries. Distances are summed geodesic geometry lengths from the validated summary, not the undocumented source-distance fields. The violin shapes estimate per-area Gaussian probability density with the same pooled robust Silverman bandwidth (33.512 m). They stop at each area’s observed minimum and maximum; width indicates estimated density, not sample count. All original dots retain exact metre positions, and labelled marks show area medians. CLUE polygons are municipal analysis areas, not official suburbs.

Forest activity flags indicate documentation: empty source flags remain “not recorded”. The 13 exact combinations sum to 532. The site version was extracted on 12 October 2026; this date does not mean every site was surveyed then. No live closure, accessibility, fee or availability information is claimed.

National charts use published historical NVS estimates for Australian residents aged 15 and over, calendar years ending December. Purpose night shares divide by published Total nights; purpose trip estimates overlap and are not added. No newer DoTS observations are spliced into the series. These are historical snapshots, not a current visitor guide. Municipal processing dates and national survey years differ.

Local maps use Mercator at a city scale. Background streets are City of Melbourne’s Street names lines (source September 2021, processed November 2022). Water polygons come from Vicmap Hydro, extracted 14 October 2026. Green shading shows VPA historical open-space polygons recorded as open public parks, gardens, recreation corridors, sport or natural space; that inventory is not maintained and does not prove current access. Background features are clipped and lightly simplified for display; landmark labels use the existing municipal inventory coordinates. These layers give orientation, not turn-by-turn navigation. Sources, licences and transformations. The national map uses an Australia-centred Albers equal-area projection and a shared 0–100% sequential colour scale. Tiny ACT is marked at its reference location, with full state figures retained in the repository data; geography is not resized. State boundaries are simplified for display, with very small offshore components omitted; original geometry remains in the repository (display geometry report). Generalised state polygons have been oriented for Vega; numerically collapsed parts were omitted from display geometry (geometry report). The scatter’s two domains and inner plot dimensions are identical.
